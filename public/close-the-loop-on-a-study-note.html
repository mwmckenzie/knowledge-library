Close the loop on a study note

Rereading a note tells you what you recognize while it is in view. A better check for recall starts with the note closed and a question that has a verifiable answer. This worksheet turns the feedback loop described in the library's essay on learning and information into a small exercise you can repeat with any reference.

Ask for something you can check

Pick one note and write an application question. Replace “Do I understand the rule?” with “Which of these two examples satisfies the rule, and why?” Write where you will verify the answer before attempting the question. If the note disagrees with a primary source, record the discrepancy and check which version or conditions each one describes.

Record your answer before you check it

  1. Hide the note and answer in one or two sentences.
  2. Mark your confidence as low, medium, or high before looking up the answer.
  3. Open the source. Record the specific condition you missed or the evidence that supported your answer.
  4. Revise the note in one sentence, keeping the original question for tomorrow.
  5. Next time, answer again with a different example. Use the same rule and say why it applies.

Keep a wrong answer with high confidence on the review list. It flags a misconception that may survive a quick reread. If your answer was correct and confidence was low, write down the piece of evidence that would make you more confident next time.

Try a worked example

A fictional reading group uses this rule: a source card is complete when it has a title, a retrieval date, and either a section heading or a page number. Close the note for a moment and decide which card is complete.

  • Card A gives the title, retrieval date, and the URL of the homepage.
  • Card B gives the title, retrieval date, and page 14.
Reveal and compare the answer

Card B is complete. Card A provides a way to open the site, but the rule requires a specific location inside the source. A useful revision is “add a heading or page number so another reader can locate the passage.” Try a new card with a title, retrieval date, and an exact heading to check that you can apply the other option.
